Why my idea doesn't actually work (yet)

October 2026

I bumped into a classic problem for most integration architectures using APIs and which are using queues to talk to each other. What fails from one to another will end up in dead letter queues, messages that won't be so important unless you decide to do something about it. Those messages might be useful to reprocess if, for a reason, they failed.

What is the pain? The pain really depends on your line of business. Let me give you a couple of examples. It can be a payment not going well because of an unsupported currency or invalid card numbers. It can be an order placed on your e-commerce that, for some reason, when it's time to deduct the stock from your external warehouse, this one doesn't have stock anymore.

Downtime is expensive. Companies don't want just another tool to use and pay for.
